Framework 1 of 7

The Mom Test

1

Customer interview framework that helps you ask questions that reveal truth, even from people trying to be nice.

The Mom Test is about asking the right questions. Don't ask "Would you buy my product?" Instead ask about their life,
their problems, and what they currently do. Talk about their life instead of your idea. Ask about specifics in the past
instead of generics or opinions about the future. Talk less and listen more.

The Mom Test Golden Rules

👂

Talk About Their Life

Not your idea. Ask about their problems, not your solution.

🕐

Ask About the Past

Not hypotheticals. "When was the last time..." beats "Would you ever..."

🤐

Talk Less, Listen More

You're there to learn, not to pitch. Stay curious.

❌ Bad Questions

  • "Would you buy this product?"

    → People lie about future behavior

  • "Do you think this is a good idea?"

    → Seeking compliments, not truth

  • "Would you pay $X for this?"

    → Hypothetical commitment means nothing

✅ Good Questions

  • "When was the last time you encountered [problem]?"

    → Validates the problem exists

  • "What are you currently using to solve this?"

    → Reveals real behavior and budget

  • "Can you walk me through how you do [task] today?"

    → Uncovers actual workflow and pain

Interview Script Template

1. Set the Stage (2 min)

"Thanks for meeting with me. I'm researching [problem area] and would love to learn about your experience. Nothing to sell today - just want to understand how you work."

2. Understand Their World (10 min)

  • "Tell me about your role and what a typical day looks like."
  • "What are the biggest challenges you face with [problem area]?"
  • "When was the last time you dealt with [specific problem]?"
  • "Walk me through exactly what happened."

3. Dig into Current Solutions (8 min)

  • "What have you tried to solve this?"
  • "What are you currently using? Show me."
  • "What do you like about it? What frustrates you?"
  • "How much does it cost? Who approved that purchase?"

4. Explore Their Process (8 min)

  • "Can you show me how you do [task] right now?"
  • "What happens when things go wrong?"
  • "How do other people on your team handle this?"
  • "What would make this significantly better for you?"

5. The Commitment Ask (2 min)

"I'm building something that might help with [their specific pain]. Would you be willing to try an early version?" or "Can you introduce me to others who face this problem?"

Note: Only ask if the conversation went well and they clearly have the problem.

Signals to Watch For

✅ Good Signs

  • • They complain without prompting
  • • They show you their current solution
  • • They've already spent money/time trying to solve it
  • • They ask when they can use your solution
  • • They offer to introduce you to others

❌ Red Flags

  • • "That's an interesting idea" (polite dismissal)
  • • They say they'd "probably" use it
  • • They ask you to keep them posted
  • • They have workarounds they're happy with
  • • They've never paid for anything similar

Framework 2 of 7

Distribution Channel Testing

2

Test 3-5 acquisition channels quickly to find what works for your product. Focus beats spreading thin.

Most startups fail because they build something nobody wants, or they build something people want but can't figure out
how to reach them. Channel testing is about rapidly testing different distribution methods to find your primary acquisition channel.

Test these in parallel (1-2 weeks each): Content marketing (blog, SEO), Social media (Twitter, LinkedIn, Reddit),
Product Hunt / Hacker News launches, Cold outreach (email, LinkedIn), Paid ads (Google, Meta, $100 budget),
Community building (Discord, Slack), Partnerships and integrations, Word of mouth / referrals.

For each channel, measure: Traffic/impressions, Signups, Activated users, Cost (time and money), Quality of users.
Double down on what works. Kill what doesn't.

8 Channels to Test (Pick 3-5)

1. Content Marketing (SEO + Blog)

Write 10 articles targeting high-intent keywords. Track organic traffic and signups.

Timeline: 4-8 weeks • Cost: Time + $0-50 (tools) • Best for: B2B SaaS, info products

2. Social Media (Twitter/LinkedIn/Reddit)

Post daily for 2 weeks. Share insights, not pitches. Drive to landing page.

Timeline: 2-4 weeks • Cost: Time • Best for: Dev tools, B2B, productivity

3. Product Hunt / Hacker News Launch

One-time spike. Prep for launch day. Reply to all comments. Capture emails.

Timeline: 1 day + prep • Cost: Time • Best for: Tech products, tools, apps

4. Cold Outreach (Email/LinkedIn)

Send 50-100 personalized messages per day. Track open, response, and conversion rates.

Timeline: 1-2 weeks • Cost: Time + $50 (tools) • Best for: B2B, high-ticket services

5. Paid Ads (Google/Meta)

Start with $100 budget. Test 3 ad variations. Measure CPA and quality of signups.

Timeline: 1 week • Cost: $100-500 • Best for: Products with clear value prop

6. Community Building (Discord/Slack)

Create a community around your niche. Provide value first, sell second.

Timeline: 4+ weeks • Cost: Time • Best for: Creator tools, platforms, courses

7. Partnerships & Integrations

Partner with complementary products. Cross-promote. Build integrations.

Timeline: 2-4 weeks • Cost: Time + rev share • Best for: B2B SaaS, marketplaces

8. Word of Mouth / Referrals

Build a referral program. "Give $20, Get $20" or free month for referrals.

Timeline: Ongoing • Cost: Credits/discounts • Best for: Products with network effects

How to Test Each Channel

1

Set Budget & Timeline

Commit 1-2 weeks and $0-200 per channel. Don't quit too early.

2

Track Key Metrics

Impressions → Clicks → Signups → Activated Users. Calculate CPA.

3

Measure User Quality

Not just quantity. Do they activate? Retain? Refer others? Pay?

4

Double Down or Kill

If a channel works, 10x it. If it doesn't, kill it and move to the next.

Conversion Copywriting

3

Framework for writing clear, compelling copy that converts visitors into customers using proven psychological triggers.

Great copy follows a formula: capture attention with a clear headline, articulate the problem, present your solution,
show proof it works, overcome objections, and create urgency. Use frameworks like PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution),
AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action), or the Before-After-Bridge method.

PAS Framework

Problem

Identify the pain point clearly

"Managing your team's time off is a spreadsheet nightmare."

Agitate

Make the problem feel urgent

"Double bookings. Lost requests. Angry employees. You're stuck playing email ping-pong."

Solution

Present your product as the answer

"TimeOff makes leave management effortless. One click approvals, automated sync, zero spreadsheets."

AIDA Framework

Attention

Stop the scroll with a hook

"Hiring developers takes 90 days. We do it in 48 hours."

Interest

Show how it works

"Browse 500+ pre-vetted profiles. Message instantly. Start interviews today."

Desire

Paint the outcome

"Skip the 6-week recruiting slog. Get back to building your product."

Action

Clear call-to-action

"Browse developers now →"

Before-After-Bridge

Before

Describe their current pain

"Right now, you're manually tracking invoices in spreadsheets. It takes hours each week, mistakes slip through, and clients get frustrated with late bills."

After

Show the transformation

"Imagine invoices that send themselves on time, every time. Payments tracked automatically. No more spreadsheet hell. Just effortless billing."

Bridge

Your product is the bridge

"InvoiceFlow automates your entire billing workflow. Set it once, forget it forever. Join 5,000+ freelancers who bill on autopilot."

Proven Headline Formulas

[Do Something Hard] in [Short Time]

"Build a landing page in 10 minutes"

[Achieve Outcome] Without [Common Pain]

"Grow your email list without annoying popups"

The [Adjective] Way to [Achieve Goal]

"The fastest way to find product-market fit"

[Number] [Ways/Secrets/Tips] to [Benefit]

"7 proven ways to double your conversion rate"

Get [Benefit] in [Timeframe] or [Guarantee]

"Get your first customer in 30 days or your money back"

Power Words That Convert

Results

Use sparingly. One power word per headline. Overuse kills credibility.

Framework 4 of 7

Founder-Led Sales

4

Close your first deals yourself. High-touch sales teach you positioning, objections, and what value resonates.

The founder must sell the first 10-50 deals. Not because you're good at sales, but because you need to learn what actually
makes people buy. Every objection teaches you something about your positioning. Every "yes" tells you what value resonates.

Process: Identify 20 target customers (warm intros > cold outreach). Book 30-min calls (not demos yet - discovery).
Ask about their current solution, pain points, budget, decision process. Present solution (15 mins max).
Handle objections (write them all down). Close or set next steps. Follow up relentlessly.

Early pricing tips: Charge something, even if low. Test 3 price points with different segments. Annual > monthly for cash flow.
Offer "founding customer" pricing with locked-in rates. Always ask "what would make this a no-brainer?"

Your First Sales Process

1

Build a List of 20 Target Customers

Be specific: name, company, role, why they'd want this. Warm intros > cold outreach.

Example: "Emma Chen, Head of Marketing at Series A SaaS co, struggles with content ROI tracking"

2

Outreach Message (Keep it Short)

3 sentences max. Problem → Solution → Ask for 15 min call.

"Hi [Name], saw you're working on [problem]. We built [product] that helps [outcome]. Would love to show you a quick demo - are you free for 15 min this week?"

3

Discovery Call (30 min)

Spend 70% listening, 30% talking. Understand their world first.

  • • "Walk me through your current process for [problem]"
  • • "What's the biggest pain point you face?"
  • • "How much does this problem cost you? (time/money)"
  • • "What have you tried so far?"
  • • "Who else is involved in this decision?"
4

Present Solution (15 min)

Tailor your demo to THEIR specific problem you just learned about.

Structure: Recap their problem → Show how your product solves it → Share 1-2 customer examples → Pricing.

5

Handle Objections

Write down EVERY objection. They're teaching you what's broken in your positioning.

"Too expensive" → Ask: "Compared to what? What's your budget?" Might be positioning issue.

"Need to think about it" → Ask: "What specifically do you need to think about?" Uncover real objection.

"Not the right time" → Ask: "When would be the right time? What needs to happen first?"

6

Close or Set Next Steps

Always leave with a concrete next action. Never end with "I'll follow up."

Examples: "Let's get you set up - I'll send onboarding link" or "I'll send proposal by Friday, can we review together Monday at 2pm?"

7

Follow Up Relentlessly

80% of sales happen after the 5th follow-up. Most founders give up after 1-2.

Cadence: Day 1 (call summary), Day 3 (check-in), Day 7 (value add content), Day 14 (final push)

Early Stage Pricing Tips

Charge Something (Even If Low)

Free users rarely give feedback. Paying customers care. Start at $29-99/mo for B2B.

Test 3 Price Points

Pitch $50/mo to first 5 prospects, $100/mo to next 5, $200/mo to last 5. See what converts.

Annual > Monthly

Offer 2 months free for annual. Improves cash flow and retention. "Pay $500/year instead of $600."

"Founding Customer" Discount

Offer locked-in rate forever. "You're getting in at $49/mo, but price goes to $99 next month."

Always Ask: "What Would Make This a No-Brainer?"

Learn what features, guarantees, or pricing would make them say yes immediately.

Framework 5 of 7

SPIN Selling Framework

5

B2B sales methodology using Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff questions to uncover customer needs.

SPIN Selling works for complex B2B sales. Situation questions establish context, Problem questions uncover difficulties,
Implication questions make the problem urgent, and Need-payoff questions get buyers to articulate the value of your solution.
Don't pitch too early - spend 70% of the call asking questions and listening.

The SPIN Methodology

SPIN Selling is designed for complex B2B sales. Instead of pitching, you ask four types of questions that lead buyers to recognize they need your solution.

Key principle: People buy when they feel the pain is urgent and your solution is the answer. SPIN questions make that happen.

S

Situation Questions

Gather context about their current setup. Keep these brief—too many and you sound like a survey.

"What's your current process for [task]?"

"How many people are on your team?"

"Which tools are you using today?"

"Who's responsible for [specific area]?"

⚠️ Warning: Limit to 3-4 questions. Move to Problem questions quickly.

P

Problem Questions

Uncover difficulties, pain points, and dissatisfactions with their current approach.

"What's frustrating about your current solution?"

"Where do bottlenecks show up in your workflow?"

"What happens when things go wrong?"

"Is [specific problem] something you deal with often?"

✅ Pro Tip: Listen for pain. When they complain, dig deeper with follow-ups.

I

Implication Questions

Make the problem feel bigger and more urgent. Connect pain to business impact.

"How does that affect your team's productivity?"

"What does that cost you in revenue?"

"If this continues, what happens in 6 months?"

"Does that create problems for other departments too?"

💡 Goal: Get them to say "This is a bigger problem than I realized."

N

Need-Payoff Questions

Get them to articulate the benefits of solving the problem. Let THEM sell it to themselves.

"If you could solve this, how would that help your team?"

"What would it be worth to you to eliminate [problem]?"

"How would faster [outcome] impact your business?"

"Would automating this free up time for more strategic work?"

🎯 Perfect: When they say "Yeah, that would be huge for us!"—now you can pitch.

Discovery Call Script (30 min)

Opening (2 min)

"Thanks for taking the time. I'd love to learn more about [their company] and see if there's a fit. I'll ask some questions about your current setup and challenges. Sound good?"

Situation (5 min)

Get context. Ask 3-4 Situation questions to understand their world.

Example flow: Current process → Team size → Tools used → Decision makers

Problem (8 min)

Dig into pain. Ask Problem questions and listen carefully. Follow up when you hear frustration.

"Tell me more about that..." "How often does that happen?" "What have you tried?"

Implication (7 min)

Make it urgent. Connect problems to business impact. Get them to feel the cost of inaction.

"What's that costing you?" "How does that affect other teams?" "What if this isn't fixed?"

Need-Payoff (6 min)

Let them sell themselves. Ask about benefits of solving the problem. They should be excited by now.

"If we could solve this, what would that mean for you?" "How would that change things?"

Transition to Demo (2 min)

NOW you can pitch. Frame your solution around THEIR words.

"Based on what you've shared, let me show you how we solve exactly that problem..."

Common SPIN Mistakes

❌

Pitching too early before understanding their pain

❌

Asking too many Situation questions (feels like an interrogation)

❌

Not listening—just waiting for your turn to talk

❌

Skipping Need-Payoff questions (you end up selling, not them)

❌

Reading from a script instead of having a conversation

Framework 6 of 7

Early Retention Focus

6

Keep the users you get. At MVP stage, 10 retained users > 100 churned users. Build for your power users.

Retention is the most important metric at MVP stage. If users don't come back, nothing else matters. Don't focus on acquisition
until you have good retention. Benchmark: Day 1: 40%+, Week 1: 25%+, Month 1: 15%+ (varies by product).

How to improve retention: Talk to churned users (why'd you leave?). Talk to power users (why do you stay?).
Find the "aha moment" - what action predicts retention? Double down on getting users to that moment faster.
Manual onboarding for first 50 users. Weekly check-ins. Build features power users request.

Retention tactics: Onboarding emails (day 0, 3, 7). Push notifications for key actions. Weekly digest emails.
In-app prompts to complete core actions. Personal outreach when users go quiet. Make your product habit-forming.

Why Retention > Acquisition

If users don't come back, you're filling a leaky bucket. Don't focus on growth until you have good retention. 10 users who love your product > 100 users who tried it once and left.

Retention Benchmarks (by product type):

  • • Day 1: 40%+ (great), 25-40% (okay), <25% (fix this first)
  • • Week 1: 25%+ (great), 15-25% (okay), <15% (broken)
  • • Month 1: 15%+ (great), 10-15% (okay), <10% (pivot or die)

Find Your "Aha Moment"

The aha moment is the action that predicts retention. Users who do X are 3x more likely to stick around.

Famous Aha Moments

  • • Facebook: Add 7 friends in 10 days
  • • Slack: Send 2,000 team messages
  • • Dropbox: Put 1 file in 1 folder on 1 device
  • • Twitter: Follow 30 accounts
  • • Airbnb: Complete a booking

How to Find Yours

Step 1: Compare retained users vs churned users

What did retained users do that churned users didn't?

Step 2: Look for patterns in first session

What action happens within the first hour/day that matters?

Step 3: Test different thresholds

Is it 1 action? 3 actions? 10 actions? Find the magic number.

Step 4: Optimize onboarding to get users there fast

Remove friction, add prompts, celebrate the moment

Retention Tactics That Work

1. Manual Onboarding (First 50 Users)

Book 30-min onboarding calls. Walk them through setup. Get them to aha moment in first session.

Scalable? No. Worth it? Absolutely. You'll learn why people succeed or fail.

2. Onboarding Email Sequence

Day 0: Welcome + quick win. Day 3: Feature tip. Day 7: Success story. Day 14: Check-in.

Tools: Customer.io, Loops, or plain Gmail for first 50 users

3. In-App Prompts to Aha Moment

Tooltips, checklists, progress bars that guide users to complete the key action.

Example: "2/5 steps complete - Invite your team to unlock collaboration features"

4. Weekly Check-Ins with Power Users

Schedule recurring calls with your top 10 users. Ask: What's working? What's frustrating? What would make you use this daily?

These users will tell you what to build next.

5. Re-Engagement Campaigns

When users go quiet (3-7 days inactive), send personal email: "Hey [Name], noticed you haven't been back. What can I help with?"

Response rate: 20-40%. You'll learn why they stopped using it.

6. Build for Power Users, Not Everyone

Find your 10% power users. Build features they request. They'll become evangelists and recruit others like them.

Don't try to please everyone at MVP stage. Go deep with your core audience.

7. Habit Formation Triggers

Push notifications, digest emails, reminders. Make your product part of their routine.

Example: "Your weekly report is ready" (every Monday 9am), "3 new matches found" (triggers curiosity)

8. Talk to Churned Users

Call or email every user who churns. Ask: "Why did you stop using [product]? What would bring you back?"

This feedback is more valuable than feature requests from active users.

Notice retention improving week-over-week? That's product-market fit emerging.

Questions to Ask Retained Users

  • • "What would you do if you couldn't use [product] anymore?"
  • • "How often do you use [product]? Daily? Weekly? Why?"
  • • "What's the main reason you keep coming back?"
  • • "What almost made you stop using it? What kept you going?"
  • • "If you could add one feature, what would make this 10x better?"
  • • "Who else do you know who has this same problem?"

Framework 7 of 7

Growth & Validation Loops

7

Build systems to continuously test assumptions, measure what matters, and create feedback loops that drive sustainable growth.

Growth isn't about hacks - it's about building loops. A growth loop is where an input generates an output that creates more input.
Examples: User invites friends (viral loop), content generates SEO traffic (content loop), paid ads fund more ads (paid loop).
Combine with validation: every loop should test an assumption and generate learning.

What Are Growth Loops?

A growth loop is a system where an output becomes an input. Unlike linear funnels (spend money → get customers → done), loops create compounding growth: each user/action creates more users/actions.

Example: User invites friend → Friend signs up → Friend invites their friend → More signups (Viral Loop)

🔁

1. Viral Loop

Users bring more users

How It Works:

User signs up → Gets value → Invites friends (because it's better together) → Friends sign up → Repeat

✅ Good Examples

  • • Dropbox: Get extra storage for referrals
  • • Notion: Invite team = shared workspace
  • • WhatsApp: More friends = more value
  • • Calendly: Each meeting invite is an ad

🔑 Key Metrics

  • • K-factor: Invites per user
  • • Conversion: % who accept invite
  • • Cycle time: Days from signup to invite

Build This If: Your product is multiplayer, collaborative, or has network effects.

📝

2. Content Loop (SEO)

Content brings users who create more content

How It Works:

Create content → Ranks on Google → Drives traffic → Users create more content → More pages rank → Repeat

✅ Good Examples

  • • Pinterest: Pins rank, drive signups, users pin more
  • • Yelp: Reviews rank for "[city] restaurants"
  • • Stack Overflow: Q&A ranks for tech problems
  • • TripAdvisor: Hotel reviews = SEO goldmine

🔑 Key Metrics

  • • Pages indexed: Google coverage
  • • Organic traffic: Monthly visits
  • • Content created: UGC per user

Build This If: Your product generates user-created content (reviews, posts, listings, Q&A).

💰

3. Paid Loop

Revenue funds more customer acquisition

How It Works:

Spend $100 on ads → Acquire customer → Customer pays $300 → Reinvest $300 in ads → Acquire 3 customers → Repeat

✅ Works When:

  • • LTV significantly higher than CAC (3:1+ ratio)
  • • Fast payback period (< 6 months)
  • • Repeatable ad performance
  • • Strong retention (keep customers long-term)

🔑 Key Metrics

  • • CAC: Cost to acquire customer
  • • LTV: Lifetime value per customer
  • • Payback: Months to recover CAC

⚠️ Warning: Don't start with paid ads until you have proven retention and unit economics.

How to Build Your First Growth Loop

1

Map Your Current User Journey

Write down every step from awareness to active user. Look for natural sharing moments.

2

Identify One Loop Type That Fits

Pick viral if multiplayer, content if UGC-driven, paid if you have strong unit economics.

3

Build the Smallest Version

Don't over-engineer. Simple invite button, basic SEO, small ad budget. Ship fast.

4

Measure Loop Velocity

How long from input to output? Shorten cycle time by removing friction.

5

Optimize One Variable at a Time

Increase k-factor OR shorten cycle time OR improve conversion. Don't change everything at once.
